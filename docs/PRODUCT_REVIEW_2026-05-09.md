# Product Review — 2026-05-09

Owner: Aurelith, Product / Game Design Strategist

## Signal Summary

The latest iPhone playtest produced real product signal. Logan skipped roughly 10–20 minutes of normal Essence waiting to reach later moments faster, so this should not be treated as clean balance evidence. It **does** strongly indicate that the first-session experience needs more visible activity, stronger presentation beats, and clearer emotional payoff.

## What the notes are really saying

The game systems exist, but several of them are not being *felt* strongly enough:

- Hatching exists, but the egg load/opening moment feels slow/static.
- Combat exists, but without enemies/animations it does not feel fun.
- Evolution exists, but it does not yet feel game-changing.
- Dragon art exists, but the white-border/background integration breaks immersion.
- Progression exists, but after checking menus and buying upgrades the player enters wait mode.

This is a classic vertical-slice polish problem: the mechanics are present, but the player-facing feedback layer has not caught up.

## What not to change yet

Do not immediately change:

1. Evolution costs / early balance pacing.
2. Rebirth timing.
3. New deep combat systems.
4. New monetization or retention systems.
5. Dragon/Wyrm art generation before Drake continuity is validated.

The skipped-time playtest makes pacing notes useful but not definitive. Fix presentation and active feedback first, then retest without skipping.

## Product priorities

### Priority 1 — Make existing systems visible and satisfying

Before adding more systems, make hatching, combat, loot, and evolution feel alive. This likely creates the largest quality jump for the least design risk.

### Priority 2 — Add low-scope activity density

The early game needs something to do while waiting for Drake. This does not need to be a full mini-game yet. Good candidates:

- short side objectives
- active adventure prompts
- micro-goals tied to existing systems
- dragon interaction moments
- visible enemy/reward feedback

### Priority 3 — Strengthen companion attachment

Every early-game improvement should reinforce: “this is my dragon growing up.” Evolution and combat should support the companion fantasy, not just stats.

## Next 3 Product Questions

1. If combat had visible enemies and simple hit/defeat motion, would the waiting period feel meaningfully better?
2. What should evolution communicate: new power, new personality, new visual identity, or all three?
3. Can early activity density be solved with existing systems/micro-goals, or does the game need one new active side activity?

## Recommendation

Run a focused polish sprint before any balance revision:

1. Dragon/background integration.
2. Egg hatch feedback.
3. Visible combat feedback.
4. Evolution ceremony.
5. Early micro-goal/activity proposal.

Then run a clean 15-minute iPhone test without skipping and compare the frustration level.
