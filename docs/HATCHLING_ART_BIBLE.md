# Hatchling Art Bible

Status: production-approved v1 emotional baseline.

Canonical production filenames:

- `assets/dragons/fire_hatchling.png`
- `assets/dragons/water_hatchling.png`
- `assets/dragons/earth_hatchling.png`

Do not rename these files automatically.

## Emotional Design Philosophy

The hatchling is the emotional main character, not just a stat unit. Every art decision should help the player think: "I like my dragon."

Prioritize affection, clarity, warmth, and identity over visual density or spectacle. The dragon should feel like a magical companion growing with the player over time.

## Shape Hierarchy

Each hatchling needs one dominant silhouette feature that reads before smaller details:

- Fire: forward-curving horns.
- Water: large flowing fin silhouette.
- Earth: chunky crystal shoulder masses.

Large readable shapes come first, medium details second, tiny details minimal.

## Behavioral Silhouettes

Each element should be recognizable by motion and posture as well as color:

- Fire: restless, eager, mischievous, quick reactions.
- Water: dreamy, drifting, soft, sleepy calm.
- Earth: grounded, sturdy, warm, protective.

Movement should reinforce personality without making the asset blurry or noisy.

## Emotional Readability Rules

At a glance, each dragon should communicate both element and personality:

- Fire should feel ambitious and chaotic-cute.
- Water should feel sleepy, mystical, and gentle.
- Earth should feel stubborn, cozy, and protective.

Eyes, face direction, and posture must remain readable during idle motion.

## Thumbnail Test

Every hatchling must remain appealing and readable at 128px or smaller.

If the personality disappears at thumbnail size, simplify shapes, enlarge expressive features, reduce detail noise, or strengthen the silhouette.

## Grayscale Test

Each hatchling must remain identifiable without color.

If grayscale makes the elements feel too similar, improve silhouette, posture, or dominant shape hierarchy before adjusting palette.

## Visual Stress Test

Validate each hatchling under realistic gameplay conditions:

- Aura FX visible and disabled.
- HUD visible and hidden.
- Low brightness overlay.
- 128px thumbnail mode.
- Normal gameplay size.
- Motion frozen and idle motion active.
- Mystic Meadow, Ember Woods, Tide Cavern, and Stoneback Hills backgrounds.

The asset should remain emotionally readable during fast-glance phone use.

## Evolution Continuity

Future forms must feel like the same companion growing up. Evolution should create the feeling of "they grew up," not "they were replaced."

Carry forward:

- Dominant silhouette feature.
- Emotional personality.
- Posture language.
- Recognizable facial identity.
- Element-specific behavior rhythm.

## Personality Continuity

The emotional arc should mature the hatchling without erasing the bond:

- Fire: chaotic ambition -> confident pride.
- Water: dreamy mysticism -> graceful wisdom.
- Earth: stubborn comfort -> dependable protection.

## Detail Density Rules

Avoid tiny scales, small rocks, busy sparkles, or excessive edge detail if they reduce phone readability.

Use:

- Large readable shapes first.
- Medium details only where they clarify identity.
- Tiny details sparingly, never as the main read.

## Motion Tolerance

Idle motion should stay subtle:

- 5-10px visual movement maximum for standard idle bobbing.
- Breathing scale should preserve eye visibility.
- Glow and aura should not cover the face.
- Resting, waking, and greeting states should read emotionally without requiring large animation.

## Attachment-First Design Philosophy

Features, art, and animation should be judged by whether they strengthen attachment.

Prefer work that creates affection, anticipation, comfort, pride, curiosity, and recognition. Avoid art choices that create noise, confusion, or emotional distance.

## Drake Continuity Notes

Drakes must feel like the same companion grown up, not redesigned creatures.

When producing Drakes:

- Preserve dominant silhouette.
- Preserve emotional personality.
- Preserve posture language.
- Preserve recognizable facial identity.
- Scale confidence and maturity without losing familiarity.
